Can Machines Learn to Trade Momentum?
Evidence from Chinese A-Shares
================================================================================


1. DATA
-------
We use five datasets from the China Stock Market & Accounting Research (CSMAR)
database covering all Chinese A-shares from January 2001 to December 2025.
Daily data includes stock returns (Dretwd), adjusted closing prices (Adjprcwd),
circulating market capitalization (Dsmvosd), and daily turnover rates (ToverOs).
Monthly data includes CSMAR-computed returns (Mretwd) and average daily turnover
(ToverOsMAvg). Corporate listing dates are used to apply a 180-day post-IPO
exclusion filter. B-shares (2xx), BSE stocks (8xx), and SSE B-shares (9xx) are
excluded from the universe. After filtering, the final sample contains 727,997
stock-month observations across 5,432 unique stocks.


2. FEATURES
-----------
We construct 74 cross-sectional features from price and volume data only. All
features are computed from data available as of the end of month t-1 and
cross-sectionally z-scored within each month to remove market-wide effects.
Returns and turnover are winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles.

3. LABEL AND DATA SPLIT
-----------------------
The prediction target is a binary label: 1 if a stock's forward one-month return
(month t+1) exceeds the cross-sectional median that month, 0 otherwise. The
median is computed separately within each calendar month, ensuring no look-ahead
bias. The resulting label distribution is approximately 50/50. The data is split
chronologically into three non-overlapping periods:

  Train  :  2002-01 to 2015-12   (260,825 obs, 14 years)
  Val    :  2016-01 to 2019-12   (140,929 obs,  4 years)
  Test   :  2020-01 to 2025-12   (326,243 obs,  6 years)

The training period covers multiple complete market cycles including the 2008
financial crisis and the 2015 A-share bubble. The validation set is used solely
for hyperparameter selection and early stopping.


4. MODELS
---------
We compare seven models across two categories. All models use identical 74
features and the same train/validation/test split. All reported performance
metrics are strictly out-of-sample (test period only). Hyperparameters for all
models are set to standard values and fixed a priori. Tree-based models use
early stopping on the validation set solely to determine the optimal number of
boosting rounds; all architectural hyperparameters are fixed.

  Linear models:
    1. Basic Momentum  — rule-based 12-1 momentum signal via sigmoid, no training
    2. L1 Logistic     — L1-penalized logistic regression (C=0.1), StandardScaler
                         59 of 74 features retained (sparsity-inducing)
    3. L2 Logistic     — L2-penalized logistic regression (C=1.0), StandardScaler
    4. LDA             — Linear discriminant analysis, StandardScaler

  Tree ensemble models:
    5. Random Forest   — 200 trees, max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=50, sqrt features
    6. XGBoost         — max_depth=6, lr=0.05, subsample=0.8, best iter=63
    7. LightGBM        — 63 leaves, lr=0.05, feature_fraction=0.8, best iter=33

5. PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION
-------------------------
Each month, all stocks are ranked by predicted probability and assigned to
quintiles Q1 (lowest) through Q5 (highest). Equal-weighted portfolios are formed
within each quintile and held for one month. The primary evaluation metrics are
the Q5 long-only portfolio and the Q5-Q1 long-short spread. The long-short spread
return is computed as the difference between the separately annualised Q5 and Q1
returns, consistent with the Fama-French convention. Two benchmarks are reported:
the equal-weighted (EW) market index (cross-sectional mean of monthly returns)
and the value-weighted (VW) market index using beginning-of-month circulating
market capitalization as weights.

7. CONCLUSION
-------------
We find that tree ensemble models consistently outperform both market benchmarks
and linear models in predicting cross-sectional stock returns in Chinese A-shares.
Three findings stand out.

First, basic momentum fails in A-shares. The Q5-Q1 spread of the rule-based
12-1 momentum strategy is negative (-1.49%), consistent with the well-documented
momentum reversal in Chinese markets. The Q5 long-only return of +11.76% falls
below both the EW (+14.71%) and VW (+16.90%) market benchmarks.

Second, tree ensemble models recover strong predictive signal that momentum rules
miss. All three tree models achieve long-short Sharpe ratios above 1.75, compared
to 1.67-1.75 for linear models. XGBoost achieves the highest long-short Sharpe
ratio (2.04) with a maximum drawdown of only -5.10% and a long-short spread
return of +23.18%. Over the full 2002-2025 timeline, the XGBoost Q5 portfolio
delivers +41.4% annualised return with SR=1.14, against +17.1% (SR=0.53) for
the EW market.

Third, linear models are surprisingly competitive. L1 Logistic achieves a
long-short Sharpe ratio of 1.75, nearly matching Random Forest (1.76), suggesting
that much of the predictive signal in A-shares is approximately linear after
proper feature engineering and cross-sectional normalization. The L1 penalty
retains 59 of 74 features, confirming that sparse linear models can capture the
momentum signal effectively. Tree ensemble models primarily add value at the
distributional extremes — they exhibit materially lower maximum drawdowns on the
long-short spread (-5.1% to -7.2%) relative to linear models (-8.2% to -8.3%).

Feature importance analysis confirms that short-term price-volume dynamics drive
predictability. Daily returns (27.6%) and daily turnover (25.1%) together account
for over half of total LightGBM gain, with monthly returns (18.1%), monthly
turnover (12.9%), MA ratios (9.6%), and MA slopes (6.8%) providing complementary
information. The most informative individual features are the 20-day cumulative
daily turnover (dto_cum20, 7.6%) and the single-day return from approximately
one month ago (dret_cum1, 7.3%).

Overall, the evidence supports the view that machines can learn to trade momentum
in Chinese A-shares — not by amplifying classical momentum signals, but by
extracting complementary information from short-term price dynamics, trading
activity, and trend features that rule-based strategies systematically miss.
